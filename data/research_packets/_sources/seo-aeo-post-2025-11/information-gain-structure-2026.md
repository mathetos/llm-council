# Information gain and answer-first structure for citations (2026)

- URLs:
  - https://redot.global/blog/information-gain-content-strategy/ (writeup of Growth Memo 1.2M ChatGPT citation analysis)

- Accessed: 2026-07-23
- Relevance to decision class: high (affirmative on-page practices for commercial pages)
- Confidence ceiling: medium — vendor studies; directional evidence only.

## Extractable claims

1. Growth Memo 1.2M ChatGPT citations: 44.2% of citations are extracted from the first 30% of a document; bottom-line-up-front structure (direct answer in the opening of each H2/H3) is structurally advantaged. (medium)
2. Same analysis: heavily cited content averages ~20.6% named-entity density vs 5–8% in standard English text; specific named tools, brands, people, and verifiable numbers beat generic filler. (medium)
3. Discovered Labs 2M citations: prompt-content alignment (mirroring buyer question phrasing in headings, self-contained answers in first 2–3 paragraphs) is the highest-ROI on-page lever, ~3x the standardized effect of the next strongest page-level signal. (medium)
4. Same study: standard AEO checklist items (Core Web Vitals, schema, FAQ blocks, author bios, structured headings) show consistent positive effects but only as a layer on top of alignment, not a substitute. (medium)
5. Original/proprietary data, first-hand test results, and case-study outcomes are the anchor of information gain: content that cannot be replicated elsewhere earns disproportionate citations. (medium)
