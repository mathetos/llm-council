# Google: Optimizing for generative AI features on Search

- URL: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide
- Announced: 2026-05-15 (Search Central Blog)
- Accessed: 2026-07-23
- Relevance to decision class: high

## Extractable claims

1. Generative AI features on Google Search (AI Overviews, AI Mode) are rooted in core Search ranking and quality systems; SEO best practices remain relevant. (evidence)
2. From Google Search’s perspective, optimizing for generative AI search is still SEO; “AEO” and “GEO” are third-party labels, not a separate Google discipline. (evidence)
3. Features use RAG/grounding against the Search index and may use query fan-out (concurrent related queries) to fetch supporting results. (evidence)
4. Non-commodity content with a unique point of view (first-hand experience, expert takes beyond common knowledge) is the primary long-run lever for presence in generative AI search. (evidence)
5. Creating separate pages for every query variation / fan-out primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses can violate scaled content abuse spam policy. (evidence)
6. For Google Search generative AI, you can ignore: llms.txt / special AI text files, “chunking” content for AI, rewriting content just for AI systems, seeking inauthentic mentions, and overfocusing on structured data as a generative-AI requirement. (evidence)
7. Structured data is not required for generative AI search and there is no special schema.org markup required; continue using it for rich-results eligibility as overall SEO. (evidence)
8. Measure generative AI visibility with the Generative AI performance report in Search Console. (evidence)
9. Eligibility also requires the site to be included in Search generative AI features in Search Console (alongside indexed + snippet-eligible). (evidence)
